Scarcity

Scarcity means our wants are unlimited but the resources to satisfy them are limited, so every person and every society has to choose.

Every choice has an opportunity cost: the next best thing you give up.

A country has a comparative advantage in a good when it can make that good at a lower opportunity cost than another country.

Unit 2 Economic Indicators and the Business Cycle

12–17% of the multiple-choice section · Unit 2 terms
Topic 2.1

The Circular Flow and GDP

The circular flow model shows how money, products, and resources move between households, firms, the government, and the rest of the world.

Open lab → Topic 2.2

Limitations of GDP

GDP measures the market value of final goods and services produced inside a country in a year.

Open lab → Topic 2.3

Unemployment

The unemployment rate counts people who have no job and are actively looking, as a share of the labor force.

Open lab → Topic 2.4

Price Indices and Inflation

A price index like the consumer price index (CPI) tracks what a fixed market basket of goods costs over time, compared with a base year set to 100.

Open lab → Topic 2.5

Costs of Inflation

Inflation erodes the purchasing power of money, and when it is unanticipated it moves purchasing power from some people to others.

Open lab → Topic 2.6

Real vs. Nominal GDP

Nominal GDP values output at the prices of the year it was produced, so it rises when prices rise even if the economy makes nothing extra.

Open lab → Topic 2.7

Real GDP does not grow in a straight line.

Unit 3 National Income and Price Determination

17–27% of the multiple-choice section · Unit 3 terms
Topic 3.1

Aggregate Demand

Aggregate demand (AD) is the total amount of real GDP that households, firms, the government, and foreign buyers want to buy at each price level: AD = C + I + G + Xn.

Open lab → Topic 3.2

Multipliers

One person's spending is another person's income, so a change in spending ripples through the economy and changes real GDP by more than the first dollar spent.

Open lab → Topic 3.3

Short-Run Aggregate Supply

Short-run aggregate supply (SRAS) shows how much real GDP firms produce at each price level while nominal wages and some other input prices are still stuck.

Open lab → Topic 3.4

Long-Run Aggregate Supply

Long-run aggregate supply (LRAS) shows how much real GDP the economy produces once every price and wage has adjusted.

Open lab → Topic 3.5

Equilibrium in the AD–AS Model

Short-run equilibrium is where aggregate demand crosses short-run aggregate supply.

Open lab → Topic 3.6

Changes in the AD–AS Model in the Short Run

A shock to aggregate demand or short-run aggregate supply moves the economy away from full employment.

Open lab → Topic 3.7

Long-Run Self-Adjustment

When AD shifts, the economy can land in a recessionary or inflationary gap.

Open lab → Topic 3.8

Fiscal Policy

Fiscal policy is the government's use of spending, taxes, and transfer payments to shift aggregate demand.

Open lab → Topic 3.9

Automatic Stabilizers

Automatic stabilizers are tax and transfer rules that push against the business cycle on their own, with no new law.

Topic 4.1

Financial Assets

People store wealth in financial assets such as cash, bank deposits, bonds, and stocks, and each one trades off liquidity, rate of return, and risk.

Open lab → Topic 4.2

Nominal vs. Real Interest Rates

The nominal interest rate is the rate written on the loan.

Open lab → Topic 4.3

Definition, Measurement, and Functions of Money

Money is any asset people widely accept as payment.

Open lab → Topic 4.4

Banking and the Expansion of the Money Supply

Banks keep only a fraction of their deposits as reserves and lend out the rest, and those loans create new money.

Open lab → Topic 4.5

The Money Market

The money market is where the demand for money meets the supply of money set by the central bank, and together they determine the nominal interest rate.

Open lab → Topic 4.6

Monetary Policy

Monetary policy is how the central bank changes interest rates to push aggregate demand toward full employment and stable prices.

Open lab → Topic 4.7

The Loanable Funds Market

The loanable funds market brings together savers, who supply funds, and borrowers, who demand them.

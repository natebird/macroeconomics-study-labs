C. Functions and forms of money

Commodity money vs. fiat money

Commodity moneyHas value of its own as a good, apart from its use as money. Gold and silver coins, salt, cigarettes in a prison camp.
Fiat moneyHas value because the government declares it legal tender and people accept it. U.S. dollars, euros. Not backed by gold.

2. Key ideas

Definition

Money

Money is any asset that people widely accept as payment for goods and services. Its key feature is liquidity: how quickly and cheaply you can use it to buy something without losing value.

Three jobs

Functions of money

Medium of exchange: used to buy things, so no barter. Unit of account: prices are stated in it. Store of value: holds purchasing power over time. Inflation weakens the store of value.

Narrow measure

M1

Currency held by the public, plus checkable (demand) deposits, plus savings deposits. These are the most liquid assets. Since 2020 the Fed has counted savings deposits in M1.

Broad measure

M2

M2 = M1 + small time deposits (CDs under $100,000) + retail money market mutual funds. The extra pieces are near money: easy to turn into M1, but not spendable directly.

Not money

Credit cards, stocks, vault cash

A credit card creates a loan, which you repay with money. Stocks and bonds must be sold first. Currency inside banks is reserves, so it is left out of M1 to avoid counting it twice.

Forms

Commodity vs. fiat

Commodity money has value as a good, like gold coins. Fiat money has value because the government declares it legal tender and people trust it. U.S. currency is fiat money.

4. Writing it on the FRQ

How to earn the points

  • When asked how a transaction affects M1 or M2, give the direction first ("M1 decreases, M2 does not change"), then name the components: "checkable deposits fall and money market funds rise; both are in M2."
  • Show the sum when given numbers: M1 = currency + checkable deposits + savings deposits; M2 = M1 + small time deposits + retail money market funds.
  • For a cash deposit, answer "the money supply does not change immediately." Currency in circulation falls and demand deposits rise by the same amount. Any later growth comes from bank lending (topic 4.4).
  • Name the function precisely: "medium of exchange," "unit of account," or "store of value." Tie it to the example in one sentence.
  • If a question uses the older definition (savings deposits in M2 only), the prompt will usually list the components. Follow the components the question gives you.

Common mistakes

  • Calling a credit card money. It is a short-term loan; the money is the checking balance you later use to pay it off.
  • Calling a debit card money. The card is a tool; the checkable deposit behind it is the money.
  • Counting currency in bank vaults in M1. Only currency held by the public counts.
  • Mixing up a money market deposit account (a bank savings account, in M1) with a money market mutual fund (M2 only).
  • Using "money" to mean income or wealth. A person with a large stock portfolio is wealthy but may hold little money.
  • Saying inflation weakens the medium of exchange. The standard answer is that inflation weakens money as a store of value.

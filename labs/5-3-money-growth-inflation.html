1. The quantity theory of money

The equation of exchange is M × V = P × Y. In growth rates it becomes %ΔM + %ΔV ≈ %ΔP + %ΔY. Solve for %ΔP and you get the inflation rate.

Growth-rate version, live

M = money supply, V = velocity (how many times each dollar is spent per year), P = price level, Y = real GDP. P × Y is nominal GDP.

2. Money is neutral in the long run

A one-time increase in the money supply shifts AD right. In the short run, output and prices both rise. In the long run, wages and price expectations catch up, SRAS shifts left, and only the price level stays higher. Dashed grey lines show the starting position.

3. Key ideas

MV = PY

Equation of exchange

Total spending (money times how often it is spent) equals nominal GDP (prices times real output). It is always true as an accounting identity.

Quantity theory

Money growth causes inflation

If velocity is stable and real GDP is set by real resources, then faster money growth must show up as a higher price level. More money chasing the same goods means higher prices.

Neutrality

Long-run monetary neutrality

In the long run, changes in the money supply change only nominal variables (prices, nominal wages, nominal GDP). Real GDP, unemployment, and the real interest rate return to their long-run levels.

Nominal vs. real

Measured in dollars or in stuff

Nominal values are in current dollars. Real values are adjusted for inflation. Money can change nominal values forever but changes real values only temporarily.

Classical view

Prices adjust

Classical economists argue wages and prices are flexible, so money affects only prices. Keynesians stress that sticky wages let money affect output in the short run. AP uses both: short run is sticky, long run is flexible.

Target

Why aim for low inflation

High inflation erodes purchasing power, hurts savers and lenders when it is unexpected, raises menu and shoe-leather costs, and makes planning hard. Many central banks aim for about 2%, not 0%, to leave a cushion against deflation.

5. On the FRQ

How to answer and draw it

  • For a quantity theory question, write the growth version and plug in: %ΔP ≈ %ΔM + %ΔV − %ΔY.
  • If the question says velocity and real output are constant, inflation equals money growth.
  • On the AD–AS graph, label AD₁ and AD₂, mark P₁, P₂, Y₁, Y₂ on the axes, and keep LRAS vertical at full employment output.
  • For the long run, draw SRAS₂ to the left so the new equilibrium is back on LRAS at a higher price level.
  • State the result in words: "Real output returns to full employment; only the price level is higher."

Common mistakes

  • Saying more money raises real GDP in the long run. It does not. Money is neutral in the long run.
  • Shifting LRAS when the money supply changes. LRAS depends on real resources and technology, not money.
  • Confusing nominal GDP with real GDP. Money growth raises nominal GDP; real GDP growth comes from resources and productivity.
  • Mixing up the price level and the inflation rate. A one-time money increase raises the price level once; continuing money growth raises the inflation rate.
  • Forgetting the signs: velocity growth adds to inflation, real GDP growth subtracts from it.

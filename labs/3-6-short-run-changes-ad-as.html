2. Key ideas

AD shifts

Demand shocks

A change in consumption, investment, government spending, or net exports shifts AD. When AD increases, real GDP and the price level both rise. When AD decreases, both fall. Unemployment moves opposite to real GDP.

SRAS shifts

Supply shocks

A change in input prices, nominal wages, or productivity shifts SRAS. When SRAS decreases (shifts left), the price level rises and real GDP falls. When SRAS increases (shifts right), the price level falls and real GDP rises.

AD right

Demand-pull inflation

Inflation caused by an increase in aggregate demand. Buyers want more output than the economy produces at the old price level, so prices are pulled up. Real GDP rises at the same time.

SRAS left

Cost-push inflation

Inflation caused by higher production costs, such as an oil price spike or higher nominal wages. Firms produce less at each price level, so the price level rises while real GDP falls.

Worst of both

Stagflation

A rising price level together with falling real GDP and rising unemployment. Only a leftward shift of SRAS produces both on its own. A demand shock always moves the price level and real GDP in the same direction.

Two shifts

One result is indeterminate

When AD and SRAS shift at the same time, you know the direction of only one of real GDP or price level. The other depends on which shift is bigger. On the AP exam, call it indeterminate.

4. Drawing it on the FRQ

Labels that earn points

  • Vertical axis: Price level. Horizontal axis: Real GDP (or real output). Do not write "Price" and "Quantity".
  • Draw a vertical LRAS at full-employment output and label Yf on the horizontal axis. Draw AD and SRAS crossing on LRAS when the question says the economy starts in long-run equilibrium.
  • Label the new curve (AD₂ or SRAS₂) and draw an arrow showing the direction of the shift.
  • Mark the new equilibrium and label both axes: Y₂ and PL₂. Graders check that Y₂ and PL₂ sit on the correct side of Yf and PL₁.
  • When asked about unemployment, compare it with the natural rate: output below Yf means unemployment is above the natural rate.
  • When both curves shift, state the one that is certain and write "indeterminate" for the other.

Common mistakes

  • Shifting AD for an oil price spike or a wage increase. Production costs shift SRAS.
  • Shifting a curve because the price level changed. A change in the price level is a movement along AD and SRAS.
  • Mixing up the words. A decrease in SRAS is a shift left (up). An increase is a shift right (down).
  • Saying "stays the same" when two shifts make a variable indeterminate. Without sizes, you cannot know.
  • Shifting LRAS for a short-run shock. An oil price spike or a burst of consumer optimism leaves LRAS where it is.
  • Jumping to the long run. Topic 3.6 questions ask about the short run. Show self-adjustment only when the question asks for it.

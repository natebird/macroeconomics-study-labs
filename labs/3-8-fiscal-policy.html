2. Key ideas

Two directions

Expansionary vs. contractionary

Expansionary fiscal policy raises government spending, cuts taxes, or raises transfers. AD shifts right, real GDP and the price level rise. Use it for a recessionary gap. Contractionary fiscal policy does the opposite. AD shifts left, real GDP and the price level fall. Use it for an inflationary gap.

Three multipliers

Why the tax multiplier is smaller

Government spending is part of AD, so every dollar counts in the first round: spending multiplier = 1/MPS. A tax cut or transfer first raises disposable income, and households spend only the MPC share of it. That skips one round, so the tax multiplier is −MPC/MPS and the transfer multiplier is MPC/MPS.

The AP formula

Gap ÷ multiplier

To find the change in a tool that closes a gap, divide the gap by that tool's multiplier. A $200 billion recessionary gap with MPC 0.75 needs a $50 billion increase in G (200 ÷ 4) or a tax change of 200 ÷ (−3) = −$66.7 billion, a tax cut.

Who decides

Discretionary fiscal policy

Discretionary fiscal policy is a deliberate decision by lawmakers, such as passing a spending bill or a tax cut. Automatic stabilizers (topic 3.9) change on their own as income rises and falls. The central bank does not run fiscal policy; that is monetary policy.

Timing

Policy lags

Policy lags are delays. The recognition lag is the time it takes to see the problem. The decision lag is the time lawmakers need to agree. The implementation lag is the time before the money is spent and affects AD. By the time a policy hits, the gap may have changed, so it can overshoot.

The budget

Effect on the budget balance

Budget balance = tax revenue − government spending − transfers. Expansionary policy moves the budget toward a budget deficit (a bigger deficit or smaller surplus). Contractionary policy moves it toward a budget surplus. Raising G and taxes by the same amount leaves the balance unchanged but still shifts AD right, because the balanced-budget multiplier is 1.

4. Fiscal policy on the FRQ

Tips that earn points

  • Label the axes Real GDP (horizontal) and Price level (vertical). Draw LRAS vertical at Yf, SRAS sloping up, AD sloping down.
  • Show the gap: mark Y1 left of Yf for a recessionary gap or right of Yf for an inflationary gap.
  • Draw AD2 with an arrow showing the shift, and label the new real GDP and price level (Y2, PL2).
  • Name a specific action and its direction: "increase government spending" or "decrease personal income taxes." "Use fiscal policy" earns nothing.
  • Show the calculation: MPS = 1 − MPC, multiplier = 1/MPS, then ΔG = gap ÷ multiplier. Graders want the number and the direction.
  • For taxes, use −MPC/MPS and state the answer in words: "decrease taxes by $66.7 billion."

Common mistakes

  • Using the spending multiplier for a tax or transfer change. Transfers use MPC/MPS and taxes use −MPC/MPS; both are one smaller in absolute value than 1/MPS.
  • Multiplying the gap by the multiplier. The gap is the change in AD you want, so divide the gap by the multiplier to get the policy change.
  • Treating transfers like government purchases. Transfers are not part of G; they work through disposable income, like a tax cut.
  • Saying the central bank changes taxes or spending. Fiscal policy belongs to Congress and the President.
  • Forgetting the price level. Expansionary policy raises both real GDP and the price level in the short run.
  • Mixing up deficit and debt. Expansionary policy moves the budget toward deficit (a larger deficit or a smaller surplus). Any deficit then adds to the national debt.

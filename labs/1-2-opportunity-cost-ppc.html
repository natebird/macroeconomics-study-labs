2. Key ideas

Definition

Opportunity cost

The value of the next best alternative you give up when you make a choice. On a PPC, the opportunity cost of more of one good is the amount of the other good the economy must stop making.

On, inside, outside

Reading the PPC

A point on the curve uses all resources fully and efficiently. A point inside means unemployed or underused resources. A point outside cannot be produced with current resources and technology.

Shape

Constant vs. increasing cost

A straight-line PPC means each extra unit costs the same amount of the other good. A PPC bowed out from the origin means each extra unit costs more than the one before. That is the law of increasing opportunity cost.

Why bowed out

Resources are specialized

Resources are not equally suited to making both goods. The first workers and machines moved into pizza making are the ones best at it and worst at robots. As you keep going, you pull in robot experts who make few pizzas, so each pizza costs more robots.

Two kinds

Productive vs. allocative efficiency

Every point on the PPC is productively efficient: no resources are wasted. Only one point is allocatively efficient: the combination of goods that society values most. A point inside the curve is neither.

Outward shift

Economic growth

More or better resources (labor, capital, natural resources) or better technology shift the PPC outward. Technology that helps only one good moves only that good's intercept. Making more capital goods today shifts the PPC out farther in the future.

3. PPC cheat sheet

Memorize which events move a point and which events shift the curve. A shift needs a change in resources or technology.

Economy returns to full employmentPoint moves out to the PPCIdle resources go back to work; no opportunity cost to reach the curve

4. Drawing it on the FRQ

Labels that earn points

  • Label each axis with the name of a good (for example, Capital goods on one axis and Consumer goods on the other). Never label a PPC axis "Price" or "Quantity".
  • Draw the PPC bowed out from the origin unless the question says opportunity cost is constant. Then draw a straight line.
  • Label points with the letters the question uses and place them clearly on, inside, or outside the curve.
  • For growth, draw the new curve outside the old one, label it PPC₂, and add an arrow. For one-good technology, keep the other intercept fixed.
  • Show your work on table questions: amount given up ÷ amount gained, with units. Write "2 units of robots per pizza," not just "2."

Common mistakes

  • Shifting the PPC inward for a recession. Unemployment is a point inside the curve. The curve shifts only when resources or technology change.
  • Giving the total cost when the question asks for the cost per unit. Divide the amount given up by the amount gained.
  • Stating the opportunity cost in the wrong good. The cost of pizzas is measured in robots, and the cost of robots in pizzas.
  • Shifting both intercepts for technology that helps only one good.
  • Saying a point inside the curve is unattainable. It is attainable; it is just inefficient.
  • Calling every point on the PPC allocatively efficient. They are all productively efficient; only one is allocatively efficient.

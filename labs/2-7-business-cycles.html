2. Key ideas

Four phases

Business cycle

The short-run ups and downs of real GDP around its long-run trend. Expansion rises to a peak, contraction falls to a trough, and a new expansion begins. Cycles are irregular: no two have the same length or depth.

Trend line

Potential output

The real GDP the economy produces when unemployment equals the natural rate. It is also called full-employment output. On the cycle graph it is the upward-sloping trend line; on the AD–AS graph it is where LRAS sits.

Actual − potential

Output gap

Output gap = actual real GDP − potential real GDP. As a percent: (actual − potential) ÷ potential × 100. A negative gap is a recessionary gap. A positive gap is an inflationary gap.

Gaps and jobs

Unemployment and inflation

In a recessionary gap, cyclical unemployment pushes unemployment above the natural rate and inflation slows. In an inflationary gap, unemployment falls below the natural rate and inflation speeds up.

Rule of thumb

What counts as a recession

A recession is commonly described as two consecutive quarters of falling real GDP. That is a rule of thumb, not an official definition. In the United States, a committee at the National Bureau of Economic Research dates recessions using a broad set of indicators, including employment, income, sales, and production.

PPC link

Inside vs. on the PPC

A recession is a point inside the PPC: resources such as workers and machines sit idle. Full employment is a point on the PPC. A recession does not shift the PPC, because the economy's resources and technology have not changed.

4. Drawing it on the FRQ

Labels that earn points

  • Vertical axis: Real GDP (or real output). Horizontal axis: Time.
  • Draw potential output as an upward-sloping straight trend line and label it.
  • Label each phase on the wavy line: expansion, peak, contraction (or recession), trough.
  • Show the gap math: output gap = actual − potential. For percent, divide by potential and multiply by 100. Write the sign and say "recessionary" or "inflationary."
  • Link to other graphs when asked: a recessionary gap is AD and SRAS crossing left of LRAS, and a point inside the PPC.
  • When asked about jobs, name the type: a recession raises cyclical unemployment.

Common mistakes

  • Using nominal GDP. The business cycle tracks real GDP, so rising prices alone do not count as an expansion.
  • Thinking every expansion is an inflationary gap. Early in a recovery, real GDP rises while it is still below potential.
  • Calling the two-quarter rule the official definition. It is a common rule of thumb.
  • Saying unemployment is zero at full employment. It equals the natural rate (frictional plus structural).
  • Shifting the PPC inward for a recession. The point moves inside; the curve stays put.
  • Mixing up the peak (highest real GDP) and the trough (lowest real GDP).

2. Key ideas

Qd = Qs

Market equilibrium

Equilibrium is the price where quantity demanded equals quantity supplied. Every buyer who wants the good at that price gets it, and every seller finds a buyer. Nothing pushes the price up or down.

Price below equilibrium

Shortage

Quantity demanded is greater than quantity supplied. Buyers compete for the limited goods and sellers raise prices. As the price rises, quantity demanded falls and quantity supplied rises until the shortage is gone.

Price above equilibrium

Surplus

Quantity supplied is greater than quantity demanded. Sellers have unsold goods and cut prices. As the price falls, quantity demanded rises and quantity supplied falls until the surplus is gone.

Shift vs. move along

What a price change does

A change in the good's own price moves the market along the curves. Only a change in a determinant (income, tastes, input costs, technology, number of sellers) shifts a curve. Shortages and surpluses fix themselves through movements along the curves.

Double shift

One result is indeterminate

When demand and supply both shift, you know the direction of only one of price or quantity. The other depends on which shift is larger. Without the sizes, the AP answer is "indeterminate."

Legal limits

Price ceilings and floors

A price ceiling is a legal maximum; it binds only when set below equilibrium and causes a shortage. A price floor is a legal minimum; it binds only when set above equilibrium and causes a surplus. Either way, the quantity sold falls.

4. Drawing it on the FRQ

Labels that earn points

  • Vertical axis: Price. Horizontal axis: Quantity. Demand slopes down, supply slopes up; label them D and S.
  • Mark equilibrium with dashed lines to both axes and label P₁ and Q₁. After a shift, label the new curve D₂ or S₂, draw an arrow, and label P₂ and Q₂.
  • For a price ceiling, draw a horizontal line below equilibrium labeled Pc. For a floor, draw it above equilibrium labeled Pf.
  • Show a shortage or surplus as the horizontal distance between Qs and Qd at that price. Label both quantities on the horizontal axis.
  • Show the math: shortage = Qd − Qs; surplus = Qs − Qd. To find equilibrium from equations, set Qd = Qs and solve for P, then plug P back in for Q.
  • For a double shift with no sizes given, write "the change in price is indeterminate" (or quantity). Graders accept that word.

Common mistakes

  • Shifting a curve because the price changed. A change in the good's own price causes a change in quantity demanded or quantity supplied, a movement along the curve.
  • Saying a shortage shifts demand left or supply right. The price rises and the market moves along both curves back to equilibrium.
  • Putting a binding ceiling above equilibrium. A ceiling above equilibrium and a floor below equilibrium have no effect.
  • Saying quantity sold under a ceiling is Qd. Buyers can only buy what sellers offer, so the quantity sold is Qs. Under a floor it is Qd.
  • Giving a direction for both price and quantity in a double shift. One of them is always indeterminate unless the sizes are given.
  • Measuring a shortage vertically on the graph. It is a quantity, so it is a horizontal gap.

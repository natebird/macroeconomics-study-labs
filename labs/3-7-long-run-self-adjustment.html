2. Key ideas

Self-correction

The economy heads back to Yf

In the long run, real GDP returns to full-employment output on its own. The adjustment happens through SRAS, because nominal wages and other input prices eventually catch up with the price level.

Recessionary gap

Wages fall, SRAS shifts right

Output is below Yf and unemployment is above the natural rate. Over time, workers accept lower nominal wages and input prices fall. Lower costs shift SRAS right, the price level falls, and real GDP rises back to Yf.

Inflationary gap

Wages rise, SRAS shifts left

Output is above Yf and unemployment is below the natural rate. Workers are scarce, so nominal wages and input prices rise. Higher costs shift SRAS left, the price level rises further, and real GDP falls back to Yf.

Long run

AD changes only the price level

After self-adjustment, real GDP is back at Yf. The only lasting effect of the AD shift is a different price level: lower after a fall in AD, higher after a rise in AD.

Sticky wages

Why it can take years

Wage contracts, minimum wage laws, and workers' resistance to pay cuts slow the adjustment, especially downward. Wages are slower to fall than to rise, so recessions can drag on.

Policy

Why governments step in

Waiting can mean years of high unemployment or rising prices. Fiscal and monetary policy can shift AD back toward Yf faster, which is the case for stabilization policy in topics 3.8 and 4.6.

4. Drawing it on the FRQ

Labels that earn points

  • Vertical axis: Price level (PL). Horizontal axis: Real GDP (or real output).
  • Draw LRAS vertical and label full-employment output Yf on the horizontal axis.
  • Label the short-run equilibrium where AD crosses SRAS, with its output (Y₂) and price level (PL₂) on the axes. Show the gap between Y₂ and Yf.
  • For the long run, draw SRAS₂ and an arrow showing the shift, then label the new equilibrium on LRAS with PL₃.
  • Name the mechanism in words: "Nominal wages fall (rise), so SRAS shifts right (left)." Graders look for the word wages or input prices.
  • If asked about the long run, say real GDP returns to Yf and unemployment returns to the natural rate.

Common mistakes

  • Shifting AD during self-adjustment. With no policy, AD stays put. Only SRAS shifts.
  • Shifting LRAS. Wage and input-price changes move SRAS, not LRAS. Yf does not change.
  • Getting the price level backward. In a recessionary gap the price level falls further as SRAS shifts right. It does not climb back to PL₁.
  • Mixing up the wage direction. Recessionary gap: wages fall. Inflationary gap: wages rise.
  • Saying output "stays low forever." The AP model says output returns to Yf in the long run; the question is how long it takes.
  • Confusing self-adjustment with policy. Policy closes a gap by shifting AD; self-adjustment closes it by shifting SRAS.

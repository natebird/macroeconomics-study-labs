How to decide

  1. Not working and not looking (student, retiree, stay-at-home parent, discouraged worker): not in the labor force. This person is not unemployed at all.
  2. Looking, and the skills fit jobs that exist; it just takes time to find one (new graduate, quit to find a better job, moved): frictional.
  3. Looking, but the skills or location no longer match the jobs available (technology, a declining industry, a plant that moved): structural.
  4. Lost the job because total spending fell in a recession, and the job will come back when the economy recovers: cyclical.

2. Key ideas

Three groups

Employed, unemployed, not in the labor force

Every adult lands in one group. Employed means any paid work, even a few hours. Unemployed means no job and actively looking. Everyone else, such as students, retirees, and stay-at-home parents, is not in the labor force.

LF = E + U

Two rates, two denominators

The unemployment rate divides the unemployed by the labor force. The labor force participation rate divides the labor force by the adult population. Mixing up the denominators is the most common calculation error.

Limitation

Discouraged workers

People who want a job but have stopped looking are not in the labor force. When the unemployed give up, the unemployment rate falls even though nobody found work, so the rate understates joblessness.

Limitation

Underemployment

A worker stuck in a part-time job who wants full-time work, or a worker in a job below their skill level, counts as fully employed. The unemployment rate does not show this lost work.

Three types

Frictional, structural, cyclical

Frictional comes from the time it takes to search for a job. Structural comes from a mismatch between workers' skills or location and the jobs available. Cyclical comes from a fall in aggregate demand during a recession.

NRU = F + S

Natural rate and full employment

The natural rate of unemployment is frictional plus structural. At full employment cyclical unemployment is zero, the actual rate equals the natural rate, and real GDP equals potential output. Full employment never means zero unemployment.

4. On the exam

FRQ tips

  • Write the formula first, then plug in: "Unemployment rate = 8 ÷ (152 + 8) × 100 = 5%." Calculation points need the work shown.
  • Compute the labor force before anything else: labor force = employed + unemployed.
  • When asked how an event changes the unemployment rate, say what happens to both the numerator (unemployed) and the denominator (labor force).
  • Name the type and give the reason: "cyclical, because the worker lost the job when aggregate demand fell."
  • On an AD–AS graph, a recessionary gap (output left of LRAS) means the unemployment rate is above the natural rate. An inflationary gap means it is below.
  • When asked for a limitation of the unemployment rate, name discouraged workers or underemployment and explain the direction: the rate understates joblessness.

Common mistakes

  • Dividing the unemployed by the adult population. The denominator is the labor force.
  • Counting discouraged workers as unemployed. They are not looking, so they are not in the labor force.
  • Saying a part-time worker who wants full-time work is unemployed. Anyone with paid work is employed.
  • Saying full employment means 0% unemployment. It means cyclical unemployment is zero; frictional and structural remain.
  • Calling a job loss from new technology cyclical. Lost jobs that will not come back when the economy recovers are structural.
  • Saying a falling unemployment rate always means the job market improved. It also falls when people stop looking.

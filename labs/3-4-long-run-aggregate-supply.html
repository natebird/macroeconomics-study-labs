The Long-Run Aggregate Supply Study Lab

Long-run aggregate supply (LRAS) shows how much real GDP the economy produces once every price and wage has adjusted. It is a vertical line at full-employment output, and it moves only when the economy's resources or technology change. Use the lab to shift the PPC and LRAS together, then switch between the short run and the long run to see why LRAS ignores the price level.

2. Key ideas

Definition

Long-run aggregate supply

LRAS is a vertical line at full-employment output (Yf). It shows that in the long run the amount of real GDP the economy produces does not depend on the price level.

Why vertical

Every price adjusts

In the long run, nominal wages and other input prices have time to catch up with the price level. If all prices and wages rise 10%, firms' real costs and real revenues are unchanged, so they produce the same amount. Output depends on resources and technology, not on the price level.

Yf

Full-employment output

Also called potential output. It is the real GDP the economy produces when it uses its resources at normal, sustainable rates. Real GDP below Yf is a recessionary gap; above Yf is an inflationary gap.

u = frictional + structural

Natural rate of unemployment

At full employment, unemployment is not zero. It equals the natural rate: frictional plus structural unemployment, with zero cyclical unemployment. In a recessionary gap, cyclical unemployment pushes the rate above the natural rate.

PPC link

Same idea, two graphs

A point on the PPC matches output at LRAS: full employment. A point inside the PPC matches a recessionary gap: idle workers and machines. An outward PPC shift matches LRAS shifting right.

Shifters

What moves LRAS

Changes in the quantity or quality of resources (labor, physical capital, human capital, natural resources) and changes in technology. A change in the price level, in AD, or in input prices does not shift LRAS.

4. Drawing it on the FRQ

Labels that earn points

  • Vertical axis: Price level. Horizontal axis: Real GDP (or Real output). Never "Quantity" or "Price."
  • Draw LRAS as a vertical line and label Yf where it meets the horizontal axis.
  • To show growth, draw LRAS₂ to the right of LRAS₁, add an arrow, and label the new full-employment output Yf₂.
  • On a PPC, label both axes with goods (for example, Capital goods and Consumer goods). Show growth with an outward shift labeled PPC₂. Show unemployment with a point inside the curve.
  • When asked why LRAS is vertical, write: "In the long run, nominal wages and all other prices fully adjust, so real output depends on resources and technology, not on the price level."
  • For a recessionary gap, mark real GDP (Y₁) to the left of Yf and say unemployment is above the natural rate.

Common mistakes

  • Shifting LRAS when AD changes. A change in spending moves the economy along or away from LRAS; LRAS stays at Yf.
  • Saying full employment means 0% unemployment. At Yf, unemployment equals the natural rate.
  • Showing a recession as an inward PPC shift. A recession is a point inside the PPC; the economy's capacity has not changed.
  • Shifting LRAS for a change in nominal wages or oil prices. Input prices shift SRAS.
  • Drawing LRAS with a slope. It is vertical because output in the long run does not respond to the price level.
  • Mixing up the natural rate with cyclical unemployment. Cyclical unemployment is zero at Yf.

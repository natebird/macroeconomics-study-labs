2. Key ideas

The model

Circular flow model

Households sell land, labor, capital, and entrepreneurship to firms in the factor market and receive wages, rent, interest, and profit. They spend that income on goods and services in the product market. Money flows one way around the loop; goods, services, and factors flow the other way.

Definition

Gross domestic product (GDP)

The market value of all final goods and services produced within a country's borders in a given year. Ownership does not matter. A Japanese-owned car plant in Ohio adds to U.S. GDP.

C + I + G + Xn

Expenditure approach

Add consumption, investment, government purchases, and net exports. Imports are subtracted because spending on imports is already inside C, I, and G, and GDP counts only domestic production.

I in GDP

What investment means

Investment is spending on new capital goods (machines, tools, factories), new residential housing, and changes in business inventories. Buying stocks or bonds is a financial transaction and adds nothing to GDP.

Avoid double counting

Final goods and value added

Intermediate goods are inputs used up to make something else. Their value is already in the final price, so GDP leaves them out. Adding the value added at each stage of production gives the same total as the final sale price.

Spending = income

Income approach

Every dollar spent on final output becomes someone's income. Adding wages, rent, interest, and profit gives GDP too. Leakages (saving, taxes, imports) leave the spending stream; injections (investment, government purchases, exports) enter it.

4. On the free-response section

Tips that earn points

  • Write the formula before the numbers: GDP = C + I + G + (X − M). Then substitute and show the total. Graders look for the setup.
  • When asked whether an item counts, name the component (C, I, G, or Xn) or name the reason it is excluded, such as "transfer payment, no good or service is produced."
  • For value added, show the subtraction at each stage (sale price minus cost of intermediate inputs), then add the stages. The sum equals the final price.
  • In a circular flow drawing, label the product market and the factor market, and show money moving opposite to goods, services, and factors.
  • For unsold output, say it counts as inventory investment in the year it is produced.

Common mistakes

  • Calling stock or bond purchases "investment." In GDP, investment means new capital goods, new housing, and inventory changes.
  • Putting transfer payments into G. Social Security checks are excluded; only government purchases of goods and services are G.
  • Adding imports. Imports are subtracted. Buying an import does not itself lower GDP; the subtraction removes foreign output that was counted in C, I, or G.
  • Counting intermediate goods along with the final good. That is double counting.
  • Counting the sale price of a used item. Only the new service, such as a commission, counts.
  • Mixing up GDP and GNP. GDP is about where the output is made; GNP is about who owns the resources.

2. Key ideas

SRPC

Short-run Phillips curve

Slopes downward. In the short run, lower unemployment comes with higher inflation, and higher unemployment with lower inflation. It mirrors the upward-sloping SRAS curve.

LRPC

Long-run Phillips curve

Vertical at the natural rate of unemployment. In the long run there is no trade-off: inflation can be any level, but unemployment returns to the natural rate. It mirrors the vertical LRAS curve.

u*

Natural rate of unemployment

Frictional plus structural unemployment, with zero cyclical unemployment. At the natural rate, real GDP equals full-employment output.

πᵉ

Expected inflation

The SRPC crosses the LRPC at the expected inflation rate. When people expect more inflation, they demand higher wages, SRAS shifts left, and the SRPC shifts right.

Stagflation

Both get worse

A negative supply shock raises unemployment and inflation at the same time. On the graph, the whole SRPC shifts right (up). This is why the trade-off broke down in the 1970s.

Watch the words

Disinflation vs. deflation

Disinflation means inflation is falling but still positive (5% to 2%). Deflation means the price level itself is falling (inflation below 0%).

4. Drawing it on the FRQ

Labels that earn points

  • Vertical axis: Inflation rate. Horizontal axis: Unemployment rate.
  • Draw the LRPC vertical and label it at the natural rate (u*).
  • Draw the SRPC sloping downward and crossing the LRPC.
  • Mark the current point and label its coordinates, such as u₁ and π₁, on the axes.
  • When a curve shifts, label the new one SRPC₂ and use an arrow to show direction.

Common mistakes

  • Shifting the SRPC when AD changes. A change in AD is a movement along the SRPC.
  • Putting the price level on the vertical axis. The Phillips curve uses the inflation rate.
  • Forgetting the long-run step. If unemployment is below the natural rate, expectations rise and the SRPC shifts right until unemployment returns to u*.
  • Moving the LRPC in the same direction as LRAS. They move in opposite directions on the page.

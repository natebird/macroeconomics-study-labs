How to decide

  1. Find the market the question names. Ask: did this good's own price change, or did something else change?
  2. Own price changed: movement along supply. Price up means quantity supplied up; price down means quantity supplied down.
  3. Something else changed: shift. Ask whether sellers now want to sell more or less at every price.
  4. Cheaper inputs, better technology, more sellers, a subsidy: supply increases (right).
  5. Costlier inputs, a per-unit tax, fewer sellers, a better-paying alternative good, expecting a higher price later: supply decreases (left).
  6. A change in demand never shifts supply. If demand pushes the price up, sellers move up along their supply curve.

2. Key ideas

Definition

Supply

Supply is the whole schedule of quantities sellers are willing and able to offer at each possible price, other things held constant. Quantity supplied is one number on that schedule, at one price.

P ↑ → Qs ↑

Law of supply

Other things equal, a higher price raises the quantity supplied. A higher price makes each unit more profitable, so existing firms produce more and new output becomes worth making.

Why it slopes up

Increasing marginal cost

Producing more usually means overtime, less suitable land, and crowded equipment, so each extra unit costs more than the last. Sellers only supply those extra units if the price rises enough to cover the higher marginal cost.

Movement

Change in quantity supplied

Only a change in the good's own price causes it. The curve stays put and sellers slide to a new point on it.

Shift

Change in supply

A change in a determinant moves the whole curve. An increase in supply shifts it right (more at every price). A decrease shifts it left, which is the same as up: sellers need a higher price for each quantity.

Six determinants

What shifts supply

Input prices, technology, number of sellers, taxes and subsidies, prices of other goods the producer could make, and producer expectations about future prices.

4. Drawing it on the FRQ

Labels that earn points

  • Vertical axis: Price (or P). Horizontal axis: Quantity (or Q). Write the good's name above the graph.
  • Draw supply sloping upward and label it S (or S₁). Label the new curve S₂ and draw an arrow showing the shift.
  • For a movement, mark two points on the same curve and label P₁, Q₁ and P₂, Q₂ on the axes with dashed lines.
  • Use the exact phrases: "increase in supply" or "decrease in supply" for a shift, "increase in quantity supplied" or "decrease in quantity supplied" for a movement.
  • When asked to explain, name the determinant: "Fertilizer is an input, so a higher fertilizer price raises production costs and decreases supply."

Common mistakes

  • Saying "supply increased" when the price rose. A higher price raises the quantity supplied; the curve does not move.
  • Shifting supply right for a decrease because the curve "moves up." A decrease in supply is a shift to the left, which also looks like a shift up.
  • Shifting supply because demand changed. Buyers' incomes, tastes, and the prices of related goods for buyers shift demand.
  • Getting expectations backward. If sellers expect a higher price later, supply today decreases.
  • Getting other goods backward. If a good the producer could make instead becomes more profitable, supply of this good decreases.

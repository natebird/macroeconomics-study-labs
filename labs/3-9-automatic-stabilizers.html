In a recession, tax revenue falls and transfer payments rise; in an expansion, the reverse happens. 2. Key ideas

Definition

Automatic stabilizers

Tax and spending rules that raise aggregate demand in a recession and restrain it in an expansion without any new action by lawmakers. The main examples are the progressive income tax, unemployment insurance, and food assistance.

No new law

Built in, so they act fast

Discretionary fiscal policy needs a new bill, so it faces long recognition and decision lags. Automatic stabilizers are already written into law. They start working as soon as incomes and jobs change.

Taxes

Progressive income tax

Higher incomes face higher tax rates. When incomes fall, tax collections fall, and they fall faster than income because people drop into lower brackets. When incomes rise, collections rise faster than income.

Transfers

Unemployment and food assistance

More people qualify for unemployment insurance and food assistance when jobs disappear, so transfer payments rise in a recession. As hiring picks up, fewer people qualify and transfers fall.

Budget

Deficit in recessions, surplus in booms

Lower taxes plus higher transfers move the budget toward deficit in a recession. Higher taxes plus lower transfers move it toward surplus in an expansion. Government purchases do not have to change at all.

Size of swings

Smaller fluctuations, not none

By cushioning disposable income, stabilizers keep consumption and AD from swinging as far. AD still shifts, so recessions and booms still happen. Stabilizers reduce their size; they do not close the gap by themselves.

4. Writing it on the FRQ

Answers that earn points

  • Name the direction first, then the reason: "Tax revenue decreases because incomes fall." Graders look for the direction word (increase, decrease) and the cause.
  • Write the chain in full: lower taxes and higher transfers, so disposable income falls by less, so consumption falls by less, so AD decreases by less.
  • For the budget, write "moves toward a deficit" (or "the deficit increases"). If the budget started in surplus, the correct phrasing is that the surplus shrinks.
  • If asked to show it on an AD–AS graph, label the axes Real GDP and Price level, draw LRAS at Yf, and show AD shifting by a smaller distance. Stabilizers do not get their own curve.
  • Show your math for budget questions: budget balance = tax revenue − government purchases − transfer payments.
  • To classify a policy, say whether it needs new legislation. "Requires no new action by Congress" is the phrase that earns the point.

Common mistakes

  • Calling a new law that extends unemployment benefits an automatic stabilizer. Normal benefits are automatic; an extension voted by Congress is discretionary.
  • Saying stabilizers end recessions or close the output gap. They make the swing smaller; the gap still exists.
  • Saying the budget moves toward surplus in a recession. Revenue falls and transfers rise, so it moves toward deficit.
  • Saying a recession deficit means the government chose to spend more. Government purchases can stay the same; taxes and transfers did the moving.
  • Thinking any tax stabilizes. A lump-sum tax (the same dollar amount at every income) does not change with income, so it is not a stabilizer. Taxes that rise with income are, and progressive taxes do the most.
  • Listing central bank actions. Open market operations and interest on reserves are monetary policy, not fiscal policy.

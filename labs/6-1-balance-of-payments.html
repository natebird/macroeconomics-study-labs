2. Key ideas

Definition

Balance of payments

A record of all transactions between one country's residents and the rest of the world over a period of time. It has two main parts: the current account and the capital and financial account.

CA = trade + income + transfers

Current account

Records trade in goods and services, net investment income (interest and dividends earned abroad minus those paid to foreigners), and net transfers (gifts, aid, remittances).

Not the same number

Balance of trade vs. current account

The balance of trade is exports minus imports of goods and services. It is one piece of the current account. A trade deficit means imports are larger than exports. The current account adds net investment income and net transfers on top.

Assets

Capital and financial account

Records purchases and sales of assets: stocks, bonds, bank deposits, real estate, and factories (foreign direct investment). Official reserve transactions by central banks are included here. Foreigners buying U.S. assets is a credit; Americans buying foreign assets is a debit.

+ and −

Credits and debits

A credit brings payment into the country: exports, income received, gifts received, and asset sales to foreigners. A debit sends payment out: imports, income paid, gifts sent, and purchases of foreign assets.

CA + FA = 0

The two accounts offset

Every transaction has two sides, so the balances sum to zero. A current account deficit is matched by a capital and financial account surplus of the same size: foreigners acquire more U.S. assets than Americans acquire foreign assets.

4. Answering it on the FRQ

Moves that earn points

  • Name the account in full: "current account" or "capital and financial account". Then say whether the balance increases, decreases, or does not change.
  • Write the formula before the numbers: current account = balance of trade + net investment income + net transfers.
  • Find the balance of trade first: exports of goods and services minus imports of goods and services.
  • When given the current account, get the financial account from current account + capital and financial account = 0. Show the sign: a −$40 billion current account means a +$40 billion financial account.
  • When a question lists official reserve transactions separately, include them in the financial account total before you check that the sum is zero.
  • Explain a change in one sentence that names the flow, such as "Japanese firms buying U.S. bonds is a financial inflow, so the U.S. financial account balance increases."

Common mistakes

  • Putting dividends or interest in the financial account. Buying the stock or bond is financial; the income it pays is the current account.
  • Calling a purchase of a foreign asset a credit because the U.S. "gains" something. Payment leaves the U.S., so it is a debit.
  • Treating the balance of trade and the current account as the same number. Income and transfers can make them differ.
  • Mixing up a trade deficit with a budget deficit. A trade deficit is about imports and exports; a budget deficit is about government spending and taxes.
  • Putting remittances in the financial account. No asset changes hands, so they are transfers in the current account.
  • Saying the balance of payments is in deficit. The two accounts always sum to zero; only the parts can be in deficit or surplus.

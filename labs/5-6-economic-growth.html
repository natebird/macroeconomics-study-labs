2. Key ideas

Definition

Economic growth

An increase in potential output over time. The best measure is the percentage change in real GDP per capita, because it adjusts for both inflation and population.

Key driver

Productivity

Output per worker per hour. Living standards rise mainly when each worker can produce more, thanks to more capital, more skills, or better technology.

Graphs

PPC out, LRAS right

Growth shifts the PPC outward and the LRAS curve to the right. Both mean the same thing: the economy can now produce more at full employment.

Not growth

A point inside the PPC

Moving from a point inside the PPC to a point on it means putting idle workers and machines back to work. Output rises, but capacity did not change, so the PPC does not shift.

Trade-off

Capital goods today

Producing more capital goods now means fewer consumer goods now. The payoff is a larger outward shift of the PPC in the future.

Math

Rule of 70

Doubling time ≈ 70 ÷ growth rate. At 2% growth, real GDP per capita doubles in about 35 years. At 7%, it doubles in about 10 years.

4. Drawing it on the FRQ

Labels that earn points

  • PPC: label both axes with the goods, such as Capital goods and Consumer goods. Draw the curve bowed outward.
  • Show growth by drawing a second PPC outside the first and labeling it PPC₂.
  • AD–AS: vertical axis Price level, horizontal axis Real GDP. Draw LRAS vertical at full-employment output, labeled Yf.
  • For growth, shift LRAS right and label the new full-employment output Yf2. If the question says SRAS also shifts, draw it too.
  • Mark each equilibrium with its price level and real GDP on the axes.

Common mistakes

  • Shifting AD to show growth. Growth is a change in capacity, so it moves LRAS.
  • Calling a move from inside the PPC to the curve "growth." That is a recovery from unemployment.
  • Using nominal GDP or total GDP. Growth in living standards is measured with real GDP per capita.
  • Dividing 70 by a decimal. Use the percent: 70 ÷ 2, not 70 ÷ 0.02.
  • Forgetting the trade-off. More capital goods today means fewer consumer goods today.

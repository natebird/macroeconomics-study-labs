2. Key ideas

AD = SRAS

Short-run equilibrium

The price level and real GDP where the AD curve crosses the SRAS curve. This is where the economy actually is right now, and it can sit to the left of, on, or to the right of LRAS.

AD = SRAS = LRAS

Long-run equilibrium

All three curves cross at one point. Real GDP equals potential output (Yf), unemployment equals the natural rate, and cyclical unemployment is zero. Unemployment is not zero.

Y < Yf

Recessionary gap

AD and SRAS cross to the left of LRAS. Real GDP is below potential, so it is a negative output gap. Firms need fewer workers, so unemployment is above the natural rate.

Y > Yf

Inflationary gap

AD and SRAS cross to the right of LRAS. Real GDP is above potential, so it is a positive output gap. Firms push workers and machines past normal use, so unemployment is below the natural rate.

Y − Yf

Output gap

Output gap = actual real GDP − potential real GDP. Divide by potential and multiply by 100 to get it as a percent. A negative number is a recessionary gap; a positive number is an inflationary gap.

Read the x-axis

Output decides the gap

The type of gap depends only on where short-run output sits relative to Yf. A high price level alone does not mean an inflationary gap. An oil price shock raises the price level and still creates a recessionary gap.

4. Drawing it on the FRQ

Labels that earn points

  • Vertical axis: Price level (PL). Horizontal axis: Real GDP (or real output). Do not write "Price" and "Quantity".
  • Draw AD sloping down, SRAS sloping up, and LRAS as a vertical line. Label all three.
  • Mark full-employment output as Yf where LRAS meets the horizontal axis.
  • Label the short-run equilibrium with dashed lines to both axes: PL1 on the vertical axis and Y1 on the horizontal axis.
  • For a recessionary gap, Y1 must be clearly to the left of Yf. For an inflationary gap, clearly to the right. Graders check the position, not the shading.
  • In words, tie output to unemployment: "Real GDP is below full-employment output, so unemployment is above the natural rate."
  • If asked for the size of the gap, show the work: Y1 − Yf, with units (billions of dollars).

Common mistakes

  • Calling any high price level an inflationary gap. The gap depends on output relative to Yf. A leftward SRAS shift raises the price level and still causes a recessionary gap.
  • Saying unemployment is zero in long-run equilibrium. It equals the natural rate; only cyclical unemployment is zero.
  • Putting short-run equilibrium where AD meets LRAS. Short-run equilibrium is always AD crossing SRAS.
  • Mixing up the sign: recessionary gap is negative (Y < Yf), inflationary gap is positive (Y > Yf).
  • Drawing three curves that cross at one point when the question describes a gap. Only long-run equilibrium has all three crossing together.
  • Saying unemployment is "high" or "low" when the question asks about the natural rate. Say "above the natural rate" or "below the natural rate".

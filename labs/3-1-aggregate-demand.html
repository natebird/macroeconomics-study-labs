The Aggregate Demand Study Lab

Aggregate demand (AD) is the total amount of real GDP that households, firms, the government, and foreign buyers want to buy at each price level: AD = C + I + G + Xn. A change in the price level moves the economy along AD, while a change in anything else that affects spending shifts the whole curve. Use the lab to try both and see which part of spending changes.

2. Key ideas

AD = C + I + G + Xn

What AD measures

Aggregate demand is the quantity of real GDP that all buyers in the economy plan to purchase at each price level. It adds up consumption (C), investment (I), government spending (G), and net exports (Xn = exports − imports).

Downward slope

Three reasons AD slopes down

When the price level rises: the wealth effect lowers the purchasing power of household wealth, so C falls; the interest rate effect raises money demand and interest rates, so I falls; the exchange rate (foreign purchases) effect makes domestic goods more expensive than foreign goods, so Xn falls.

Along vs. shift

Movement along AD

The price level is on the vertical axis, so a change in the price level moves the economy along the existing AD curve. That is a change in the quantity of real GDP demanded, not a change in AD.

Determinants

What shifts AD

Anything other than the price level that changes C, I, G, or Xn shifts AD. More spending at every price level shifts AD right. Less spending at every price level shifts AD left.

Watch the cause

Interest rates both ways

If interest rates rise because the price level rose, that is the interest rate effect: a movement along AD. If interest rates rise for another reason, such as a central bank action, investment falls at every price level and AD shifts left.

Preview of 3.2

The multiplier

A first change in spending creates income for others, who then spend part of it. So the final shift in AD is larger than the first change. This lab draws the shift equal to the first change to keep the numbers simple.

4. Drawing it on the FRQ

Labels that earn points

  • Vertical axis: Price level (PL). Horizontal axis: Real GDP (or real output). Never "Price" and "Quantity".
  • Draw AD sloping down and label it AD. Label a shifted curve AD₁ or AD₂ and add an arrow showing the direction.
  • For a shift, name the component: "Government spending is a component of AD, so AD shifts right."
  • For a tax change, write the full chain: lower taxes, higher disposable income, higher consumption, AD shifts right.
  • When asked why AD slopes down, name one effect and explain it in one sentence that links the price level to C, I, or Xn.

Common mistakes

  • Shifting AD because the price level changed. A price level change is a movement along AD.
  • Using the micro reason for a downward slope (people switch to substitute goods). AD covers all goods, so the reasons are the wealth, interest rate, and exchange rate effects.
  • Saying imports add to AD. Imports are subtracted in Xn, so more imports (with exports unchanged) shift AD left.
  • Getting the currency backwards. A stronger (appreciating) dollar makes U.S. exports more expensive, so Xn falls and AD shifts left.
  • Calling a rise in the quantity of real GDP demanded an "increase in AD". An increase in AD means the whole curve shifts right.

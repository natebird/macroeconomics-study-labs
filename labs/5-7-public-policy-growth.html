2. Key ideas

Growth

Growth means more capacity

Long-run economic growth is an increase in potential output. On the graphs, LRAS shifts right and the production possibilities curve shifts outward. Pushing AD past potential is not growth.

Human capital

Education and training

Spending on schools, job training, and health makes each worker more productive. It raises AD a little now and shifts LRAS right later.

Physical capital

Infrastructure and investment

Roads, ports, broadband, and private machinery let workers produce more. An investment tax credit encourages firms to buy capital, which adds to AD now and to capacity later.

Technology

R&D and patents

Subsidies for research and patent protection reward new ideas. Better technology raises productivity, so the same inputs make more output.

Supply side

Supply-side fiscal policy

Supply-side economists argue that lower marginal tax rates and lighter regulation increase incentives to work, save, and invest, shifting aggregate supply right. Economists disagree about how large these effects are.

Laffer curve

Tax rates and tax revenue

The Laffer curve shows that revenue is zero at a 0% tax rate and also at a 100% rate, so it peaks somewhere in between. If rates are above the peak, a cut could raise revenue. Economists disagree about where the economy sits on the curve.

Link to 5.5

Crowding out can slow growth

When the government borrows, it raises the real interest rate in the loanable funds market. Private investment falls, the capital stock grows more slowly, and long-run growth is lower than it would have been.

Link to 5.6

Capital accumulation

Growth comes from more and better capital, more skilled labor, and better technology. Policy helps growth when it adds to one of these, not when it only adds spending.

Demand side

Stabilization is not growth

Expansionary monetary policy and spending at full employment raise output only for a while. In the long run the economy returns to potential, and the only lasting effect is a higher price level.

4. Answering it on the FRQ

Moves that earn points

  • Name the channel: human capital, physical capital, or technology. Then say LRAS shifts right.
  • Show growth by shifting LRAS right (label it LRAS₂) or shifting the PPC outward.
  • For a deficit-financed policy, connect the steps: government borrowing, higher real interest rate, less private investment, slower growth.
  • When AD and AS both shift right, real GDP rises but the price level change is indeterminate unless the question gives sizes.
  • Answer the time frame asked. "Short run" means AD and SRAS. "Long run" means LRAS.

Common mistakes

  • Calling any increase in real GDP "growth". A move along a fixed LRAS is a short-run boom, not growth.
  • Saying expansionary monetary policy raises long-run output. It raises only the price level in the long run.
  • Forgetting that government spending on education or infrastructure also shifts AD in the short run.
  • Claiming tax cuts always raise revenue. The Laffer curve says that happens only if rates start above the revenue-maximizing rate.
  • Shifting LRAS left for crowding out when the question asks about growth. Say growth is slower, or LRAS shifts right by less.

The Crowding Out Study Lab

When the government borrows to cover a budget deficit, it competes with businesses for the same pool of saving. That pushes up the real interest rate and squeezes out some private investment. Less investment today means less capital and slower growth later. Use the lab to follow that chain from the loanable funds market to the long run.

2. Key ideas

Definition

Crowding out

Government borrowing raises the real interest rate, which reduces private investment spending. The government's spending partly replaces private spending instead of adding to it.

The market

Loanable funds

Savers supply funds and borrowers demand them. Supply comes from private saving, public saving (a budget surplus), and foreign saving. Demand comes from firms that want to invest and from a government running a deficit.

r = i − π

Real interest rate

The loanable funds market sets the real interest rate: the nominal rate minus expected inflation. Firms compare it with the return they expect from new machines and buildings.

Public saving

Two ways to draw it

A deficit is negative public saving. You can show it as more demand for loanable funds (the government borrows) or as less supply (national saving falls). Both raise the real interest rate. AP graders accept either if you label it clearly.

Long run

Why growth slows

Investment builds the capital stock. If deficits crowd out investment year after year, the economy has fewer machines and less technology later, so LRAS shifts right more slowly.

The reverse

Crowding in

A budget surplus adds to the supply of loanable funds. The real interest rate falls, private investment rises, and long-run growth speeds up.

4. Drawing it on the FRQ

Labels that earn points

  • Vertical axis: Real interest rate. Horizontal axis: Quantity of loanable funds.
  • Draw supply sloping up and demand sloping down. Label them SLF and DLF.
  • Mark the starting rate r₁ and the new rate r₂ on the vertical axis, with r₂ above r₁ for a deficit.
  • Label the new curve DLF2 (or SLF2) and add an arrow showing the direction.
  • In words, finish the chain: higher real interest rate, lower private investment, slower growth of the capital stock.

Common mistakes

  • Using the money market. Crowding out from borrowing belongs on the loanable funds graph, which shows the real rate. The money market shows the nominal rate and a vertical money supply set by the central bank.
  • Shifting supply right for a deficit. A deficit reduces national saving, so if you shift supply it goes left.
  • Saying the quantity of loanable funds falls when demand shifts right. Total quantity rises; it is private investment that falls.
  • Mixing up deficit and debt. The deficit is one year's borrowing. The debt is the total of all past deficits minus surpluses.
  • Stopping at the interest rate. Questions about the long run want the link to capital and economic growth.
